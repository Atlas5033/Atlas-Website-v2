What to Expect From Online Suboxone Treatment in Colorado

Online Suboxone treatment allows eligible adults in Colorado to receive evaluation, medication treatment, and follow-up care through secure video appointments.

Online Suboxone treatment allows eligible adults in Colorado to receive evaluation, medication treatment, and follow-up care through secure video appointments.

At Atlas Recovery, treatment begins with a conversation—not a demand that you arrive with everything figured out. Your clinician will learn about your opioid use, health history, previous treatment, current concerns, and personal goals before recommending a plan.

Not everyone needs the same approach. Some patients can begin buprenorphine treatment at home with clinical guidance. Others may need laboratory testing, an in-person medical evaluation, withdrawal management, or a more intensive level of care based on their individual circumstances.

This article explains what the process generally looks like at Atlas Recovery. Individual recommendations may differ based on your health, safety, insurance requirements, and clinical circumstances.

Key takeaways

  • Atlas Recovery provides telehealth treatment for adults who are physically located in Colorado during their appointments.
  • Treatment begins with a free 15-minute consultation followed by a comprehensive video evaluation.
  • A drug screen is not usually completed during the initial video evaluation because patients will not yet have Atlas home-testing supplies.
  • A prescription is not guaranteed; the clinician must first determine whether buprenorphine and outpatient telehealth treatment are appropriate.
  • When clinically appropriate, patients may begin buprenorphine at home using an individualized initiation plan.
  • Follow-up visits are more frequent early in treatment and may become less frequent as treatment stabilizes.
  • Drug testing may occur through a laboratory or through structured home testing after supplies have been provided.
  • Counseling and peer support can be valuable, but participation in a particular program is not required to receive buprenorphine treatment.
  • A recurrence of opioid use should lead to a safety conversation and reassessment—not automatic punishment or discharge.

Step 1: Request a Free Consultation

The first step is a free 15-minute consultation.

This brief conversation gives you an opportunity to:

  • Describe what kind of help you are looking for
  • Ask questions about treatment
  • Learn how telehealth appointments work
  • Discuss insurance or self-pay options
  • Decide whether you would like to schedule a complete evaluation

The consultation is not the full medical evaluation, and medication is not prescribed during this call.

You do not need to be in withdrawal during the consultation. You also do not need to have stopped using opioids before reaching out.

If Atlas does not appear to be the appropriate level of care, the goal is to explain why and help identify a safer next step when possible.

Step 2: Complete Your Intake Forms

Before the initial evaluation, you will receive secure intake forms.

These forms may ask about:

  • Current and previous opioid use
  • Other substance use
  • Prior treatment with buprenorphine, methadone, or naltrexone
  • Overdose history
  • Current medications
  • Medical and mental health history
  • Allergies
  • Emergency contacts
  • Pharmacy information
  • Insurance information
  • Treatment goals
  • Telehealth consent and practice policies

Answer as honestly and completely as you can. The purpose is not to judge you or find a reason to exclude you. Accurate information helps your clinician understand risk, prevent medication interactions, and develop a safer treatment plan.

You may also be asked to provide identification and complete releases allowing Atlas to coordinate with another healthcare professional when appropriate.

Step 3: Attend Your Initial Video Evaluation

The initial evaluation is usually scheduled for approximately 60 minutes. You must be physically located in Colorado during the appointment.

At the beginning of the visit, you will be asked to confirm:

  • Your identity
  • Your current physical location
  • A telephone number where you can be reached
  • That you are in a reasonably private and safe place

Your clinician will ask about:

  • Your current and past opioid use
  • Withdrawal symptoms and cravings
  • Previous treatment experiences
  • Overdose history
  • Other prescribed medications and substances
  • Physical and mental health conditions
  • Your current supports, concerns, and treatment goals

The clinician will complete the portions of the examination that can be performed safely through video and review Colorado’s prescription drug monitoring program.

A drug screen is not usually completed during the initial video evaluation because patients generally will not yet have Atlas home-testing supplies. If testing is appropriate, the clinician will explain the next step. This may involve completing testing through a laboratory or receiving home-testing supplies after the appointment.

The evaluation is also an opportunity for you to ask questions. You do not need to know exactly what treatment you want before the visit.

An evaluation does not guarantee that a particular medication will be prescribed. Your clinician must first determine whether outpatient telehealth treatment and buprenorphine are safe and appropriate for your circumstances.

Step 4: Develop an Individualized Treatment Plan

If buprenorphine treatment is appropriate, you and your clinician will develop a plan based on factors such as:

  • The opioid you have been using
  • When you last used it
  • Your current withdrawal symptoms
  • Previous experiences with buprenorphine
  • Overdose history
  • Other medications and substances
  • Medical and psychiatric conditions
  • Your treatment preferences
  • Your ability to complete treatment safely at home

Treatment may include buprenorphine and naloxone, commonly known by the brand name Suboxone. Other buprenorphine formulations may sometimes be considered.

Your clinician will explain:

  • When to take the first dose
  • How much medication to take
  • How to evaluate withdrawal symptoms
  • What to do if symptoms improve or worsen
  • Which medication combinations create additional risk
  • When to contact Atlas
  • When emergency care is needed

Do not use someone else’s medication or follow another person’s initiation instructions. Starting buprenorphine too soon after fentanyl, heroin, oxycodone, or another opioid can cause precipitated withdrawal.

Step 5: Begin Medication When It Is Safe

Many appropriate patients can begin buprenorphine at home using written instructions and clinical guidance.

Traditional initiation usually begins after clear opioid withdrawal has developed. The timing depends on the opioid used, the time since the last use, current symptoms, and other individual factors.

Fentanyl can make this timing less predictable. Your clinician may recommend a different approach based on your history and previous treatment experiences.

During the initiation process, you may be asked to:

  • Use a withdrawal-rating guide
  • Record your symptoms
  • Record the time and amount of each dose
  • Monitor cravings and side effects
  • Avoid alcohol and nonprescribed sedating substances
  • Keep naloxone nearby
  • Contact Atlas if the plan is not working as expected

Some people need in-person withdrawal management, emergency evaluation, or a higher level of care. Telehealth initiation may not be appropriate when there is severe medical instability, severe psychiatric risk, complicated withdrawal, an unsafe environment, or another concern that cannot be managed safely through outpatient care.

Step 6: Complete an Early Follow-Up

Atlas generally schedules an early check-in after medication initiation, often within three to seven days.

The clinician will ask about:

  • Withdrawal symptoms
  • Cravings
  • Opioid or other substance use
  • Medication adherence
  • Side effects
  • Sleep and daily functioning
  • Any difficulty filling the prescription
  • Whether the current dose is providing adequate stability

The plan may be adjusted based on your response.

Contact Atlas sooner if you experience significant side effects, uncontrolled withdrawal, worsening cravings, difficulty following the initiation plan, or another nonemergency concern.

Do not wait for a routine appointment if you believe you are experiencing an emergency.

What Happens During Ongoing Follow-Up?

Follow-up appointments are generally more frequent at the beginning of treatment. Visits may become less frequent as symptoms, medication use, and treatment needs become more stable.

Follow-up care may include:

  • Reviewing withdrawal and cravings
  • Assessing how the medication is working
  • Discussing opioid and other substance use
  • Reviewing side effects and medication interactions
  • Monitoring physical and mental health
  • Reviewing prescription-monitoring information
  • Completing drug testing when appropriate
  • Confirming access to naloxone
  • Adjusting the treatment plan
  • Discussing recovery goals and additional support

Atlas provides medication management along with supportive, recovery-oriented care. Treatment is collaborative and should address the patient’s goals, safety, and overall functioning—not only a laboratory result.

Will Drug Testing Be Required?

Drug testing is commonly used during buprenorphine treatment, but patients generally will not have Atlas home-testing supplies available during the initial evaluation.

After the evaluation, Atlas may:

  • Order testing through a laboratory
  • Mail drug-testing supplies to the patient
  • Arrange a structured home test during a later telehealth appointment
  • Use previous results when they are current and clinically appropriate

Testing may help the clinician:

  • Confirm that prescribed medication is present
  • Identify unexpected substances
  • Assess medication interactions and overdose risk
  • Evaluate how well the current treatment plan is working
  • Decide whether additional support or another level of care may be needed

The type and frequency of testing depend on the patient’s clinical circumstances and, when applicable, payer requirements.

An unexpected result should begin a conversation—not automatically end treatment. The purpose of testing is to support safety and treatment decisions, not to shame or punish patients.

Will I Need Laboratory Testing?

Your clinician may order laboratory testing based on your health history, current medications, symptoms, and clinical needs.

Testing may be completed at a laboratory near you. Not every patient will need the same tests or testing schedule.

Laboratory testing should support clinical decision-making. It is not a substitute for listening to the patient, evaluating symptoms, or developing an individualized treatment plan.

Is Counseling Required?

Counseling, therapy, peer support, mutual-help groups, and recovery communities can be valuable. Atlas can help patients consider these resources when they are wanted or clinically appropriate.

However, patients are not required to prove participation in a particular counseling or recovery program before receiving buprenorphine treatment.

Medication should not be withheld solely because someone is unable or unwilling to participate in counseling. Treatment should remain individualized and responsive to the patient’s needs and goals.

If you already work with a therapist, primary care clinician, psychiatrist, recovery program, or another healthcare professional, Atlas can coordinate care with your written permission.

What If I Use Opioids During Treatment?

Using opioids during treatment does not automatically mean that treatment has failed.

It may indicate that:

  • The medication dose needs to be reassessed
  • Withdrawal or cravings are not adequately controlled
  • Medication access has been interrupted
  • The current plan does not fit the patient’s needs
  • Stress, trauma, pain, or mental health symptoms need attention
  • Additional support or another level of care may be helpful

Tell your clinician what happened as honestly as you can. The immediate priorities are reducing overdose risk, understanding what contributed to the opioid use, and deciding whether the current treatment plan remains appropriate.

Continued outpatient treatment is not always safe in every situation. A higher level of care may be recommended when risks cannot be adequately managed through telehealth. That recommendation should be explained clearly and respectfully.

How Should I Prepare for a Telehealth Appointment?

Before your appointment:

  • Choose a reasonably private location
  • Make sure you will be physically located in Colorado
  • Charge your phone, tablet, or computer
  • Test your camera and internet connection
  • Have your medication bottles or packages nearby
  • Have your pharmacy information available
  • Bring a list of medications and supplements
  • Have any requested logs, laboratory results, or testing supplies available
  • Avoid driving during the appointment
  • Write down questions you want to ask

If privacy is difficult, tell Atlas before the appointment when possible. The practice may be able to help you consider practical options.

When Is Telehealth Treatment Not Enough?

Online treatment is not designed to replace emergency care, medically supervised withdrawal management, inpatient treatment, or other intensive services when those are needed.

Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department if someone:

  • Cannot be awakened
  • Is breathing slowly, irregularly, or not at all
  • Has blue, purple, or gray lips or fingertips
  • Makes choking, snoring, or gurgling sounds and cannot be awakened
  • May be experiencing an opioid overdose
  • Has severe chest pain, severe confusion, a seizure, or another medical emergency
  • Is in immediate danger of harming themselves or someone else

Give naloxone if an opioid overdose is suspected and naloxone is available. Stay with the person and follow the emergency dispatcher’s instructions.

Call or text 988 for suicidal thoughts or an emotional crisis. Atlas Recovery is not an emergency or crisis service, and routine messages are not monitored continuously.

Common Questions

“Do I Have to Stop Using Opioids Before I Contact Atlas?”

No. You can request a consultation while you are still using opioids.

Do not make an unsafe medication change simply to prepare for an appointment. Your clinician needs accurate information about what you are using and when you last used it to help develop a safer plan.

“Will I Receive a Prescription at the First Evaluation?”

Possibly, but a prescription is not guaranteed.

The clinician must first determine whether buprenorphine is appropriate, whether it can be started safely, and whether outpatient telehealth care is the right level of treatment.

“Will I Have to Complete a Drug Screen During the First Video Visit?”

Usually not. New patients generally will not yet have Atlas home-testing supplies.

If testing is needed, the clinician may order laboratory testing or arrange for home-testing supplies after the evaluation.

“Can I Receive Treatment if I Take a Benzodiazepine?”

Taking a benzodiazepine does not automatically exclude someone from buprenorphine treatment.

However, combining buprenorphine with benzodiazepines, alcohol, sleep medications, or other sedatives can increase the risk of severe sedation and breathing problems. Your clinician must understand what you are taking and assess how those risks can be managed.

Do not suddenly stop a prescribed benzodiazepine or buprenorphine without medical guidance.

“What Happens if Telehealth Is Not the Right Level of Care?”

Your clinician will explain the concern and recommend a safer treatment setting when possible.

Needing in-person care, withdrawal management, an opioid treatment program, emergency services, or another level of support is not a failure. The goal is to match treatment to what can be provided safely and effectively.

The Bottom Line

Online Suboxone treatment can make evidence-based opioid use disorder care more accessible to adults throughout Colorado.

At Atlas Recovery, the process begins with a free consultation followed by a comprehensive video evaluation. If outpatient buprenorphine treatment is appropriate, the clinician will develop an individualized medication plan, provide guidance for beginning treatment, and arrange early and ongoing follow-up.

Patients generally do not complete an Atlas home drug screen during the initial evaluation because they have not yet received testing supplies. Testing may be arranged afterward through a laboratory or structured home testing.

Treatment is designed to support safety, stability, and meaningful progress. Patients do not need to arrive with a perfect plan, and an unexpected drug-screen result or recurrence of opioid use does not automatically end care.
