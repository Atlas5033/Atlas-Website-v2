About Atlas

Healthcare should stay human.

A small, clinician-owned practice built around thoughtful care, clear communication, and respect for the person seeking help.

Aaron Jones, MSN, PMHNP-BC, CARN-AP

Founder, Atlas Recovery

I earned my MSN from Vanderbilt University. I'm a board-certified psychiatric-mental health nurse practitioner and board certified in advanced practice addictions nursing (CARN-AP). My clinical focus is addiction treatment and recovery-focused psychiatric care.

My style is grounded in curiosity. I try to listen before assuming, ask thoughtful questions, and understand you rather than just the diagnosis in the chart. Getting to know someone is clinically valuable. When people feel respected, there is more room to talk openly about what is really going on in their lives.

I’ll offer clinical recommendations, but treatment should still be a conversation. My role is to explain the options clearly, offer my best guidance, and help build a plan that makes sense for your life.

There are different paths to recovery, and progress does not have to look the same for everyone. Recovery may include medication, therapy, rebuilding relationships, returning to work, finding community, creating stability, or simply taking the next manageable step. I do not think it is my job to define recovery for you. My job is to help you move toward the life you want.

My clinical work has included psychiatric and addiction care across crisis, hospital, and outpatient settings. I have worked with people at very different points in recovery, including those considering treatment for the first time, returning after a setback, or building long-term stability with medications such as buprenorphine.

Before becoming a nurse practitioner, I served as a Peace Corps Volunteer and later as a U.S. Army Special Forces medic. Those were very different experiences, but both shaped how I think about caring for people. They taught me how much human connection matters, especially when we’re under stress or facing uncertainty. The process matters, but the person comes first. I learned to value practical help, listening carefully, and following through.

I started Atlas because I wanted to build a practice I would trust with someone I love and a place where I would want to work myself. I wanted it to feel thoughtful, kind, and practical—a place where clinicians stay curious, people are treated with dignity, and recovery is not defined too narrowly.

Atlas is intentionally small and clinician-owned. As we grow, I want these values to show up in the everyday work of the practice—how we communicate, make decisions, and care for people.

Our treatment philosophy

People. Partnership. Excellence.

Good treatment takes sound medicine and a working relationship. These principles guide how we listen, make decisions, and respond when things get complicated.

People

Your story, strengths, relationships, values, and goals matter. We listen without judgment, including when recovery is difficult.

Partnership

You bring knowledge of your life; we bring clinical experience. We explain options and their benefits and risks, make decisions together, and set clear, reasonable expectations.

Excellence

Good care combines evidence-based judgment with warmth, curiosity, and respect. We keep learning and review what is working so care can adapt to your needs.
