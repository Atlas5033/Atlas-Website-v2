A Personal Approach

Your Patient Is in Good Hands

Referring someone for addiction treatment requires trust. Patients may feel apprehensive about discussing opioid use or starting medication. At Atlas, they have time to tell their story, ask questions, and make treatment decisions together with their clinician.

Aaron Jones, MSN, PMHNP-BC, is a Vanderbilt-trained psychiatric nurse practitioner with experience caring for people with substance use disorders across the continuum of care—from crisis response and medical triage to community-based and outpatient treatment.

Our goal is simple: when you refer someone to us, we want you to feel confident they'll receive the same care and respect they've come to expect from you.

Connected Care

You Keep Your Patient. We Supplement Their Care.

Atlas provides focused OUD treatment and recovery-focused clinical support alongside the care your patient already receives.

  • Existing care continues. Patients keep their therapist, primary care clinician, intensive outpatient program (IOP), community supports, and other ongoing care.
  • We focus on OUD treatment. Medication treatment and clinical support complement the work you’re already doing.
  • We coordinate when helpful. With patient authorization, we work with the existing team so care stays connected.

Referral Guidance

When to Refer to Atlas

Atlas provides outpatient opioid use disorder treatment for adults who can safely receive care through telehealth while located in Colorado.

Appropriate Referrals

  • Known or suspected opioid use disorder
  • Interest in starting buprenorphine/Suboxone
  • Ongoing medication for opioid use disorder (MOUD) management, including continuation of buprenorphine
  • Difficulty accessing OUD treatment locally
  • Telehealth OUD treatment while continuing care with you
  • Transition from detoxification, residential treatment, hospitalization, or another prescriber
  • Co-occurring psychiatric concerns that can be safely managed in an outpatient setting

Services

  • Opioid use disorder evaluation and individualized treatment planning
  • Buprenorphine initiation, continuation, and monitoring
  • Recovery-focused psychiatric medication management when appropriate
  • Overdose prevention and harm-reduction support
  • Care coordination with referring professionals

What Happens Next

What Happens After You Refer

We aim to contact new referrals within one business day during normal business hours. With appropriate authorization, we can share relevant updates and coordinate with you so you know what happens next.

  1. 1

    You refer

    Send the patient’s information through the secure professional referral form.

  2. 2

    We reach out

    We review the referral for clinical fit and contact the patient about services, eligibility, and scheduling.

  3. 3

    We assess

    We listen to the patient’s needs and goals and assess whether outpatient telehealth care is appropriate.

  4. 4

    We treat

    When appropriate, we develop a treatment plan together, including medication and ongoing follow-up.

  5. 5

    We stay connected

    With appropriate authorization, we communicate relevant outcomes and coordinate ongoing care with you.

Scope and Safety

When Atlas May Not Be the Right Setting

Atlas is an outpatient telehealth practice and is not an emergency, crisis, detoxification, residential, or inpatient service. Patients who are medically unstable may need in-person assessment, medically managed withdrawal, emergency evaluation, or other higher-level care before outpatient telehealth treatment is appropriate.

If you are uncertain about fit, contact Atlas before referring. If a patient is experiencing a medical or psychiatric emergency, call 911 or direct the patient to the nearest emergency department. For mental health or substance-use crisis support, call or text 988.

Access

Insurance and self-pay options

Atlas accepts select Colorado insurance plans and offers transparent self-pay options. Coverage, network participation, prior authorization, and patient responsibility depend on the individual plan and are verified before treatment whenever possible.

A referral is not required for most patients, but professional referrals are welcomed when they support continuity and coordination of care.
